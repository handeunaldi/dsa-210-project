Project overview

What the project asks

Main question

How do economic development indicators affect life expectancy across countries?

The project connects GDP per capita, healthcare expenditure, and life expectancy to understand whether economic growth improves population health directly or through stronger healthcare investment.

⌕

EDA and hypothesis testing

The analysis uses log-transformed GDP and healthcare spending, correlation analysis, group comparisons, and regional visualizations to identify broad patterns.

◈

Predictive modeling

Machine learning models predict life expectancy using economic, healthcare, temporal, and regional features, with a time-aware train/test split.

Why Random Forest fits this project

GDP and healthcare spending may have non-linear effects. Random Forest can capture threshold effects and diminishing returns better than a plain linear model.

◴

Why the time-aware split matters

The model trains on earlier years and tests on 2022–2023. This makes evaluation more realistic and reduces temporal leakage.

+

Important limitation

Large errors may come from missing factors such as education, inequality, conflict, public health quality, or healthcare efficiency.

Final interpretation

What the project shows

The visual and statistical results support the idea that countries with higher GDP per capita and higher healthcare expenditure generally have longer life expectancy. However, the relationship is not purely linear: at higher income and spending levels, gains in life expectancy become smaller.

The machine learning results strengthen this interpretation by showing that economic and healthcare indicators are useful predictors of life expectancy, while unexplained errors may point to social, political, and healthcare-system factors outside the dataset.

Website-ready takeaway

Economic growth matters, but health outcomes also depend on how effectively resources are converted into public health.
